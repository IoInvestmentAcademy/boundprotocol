## Part 1 · The structure

- [x] **1.1** Three entities. Two exist; the third is the one every cost belongs to.

| Entity | Status | Holds | Bears |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nexus Technologies Foundation** (Panama) | Incorporated | Protocol IP; ecosystem allocations; grants Token Delegation | Nothing operational |
| **BITSWIFT TECHNOLOGIES INC.** (Panama) | Incorporated | Raise proceeds; SAFT counterparty | Token legal |
| **Operating company** (EU) | To incorporate | Nothing | All operating cost |

- [ ] **1.2** The operating company is paid a **cost-plus services fee** by the Foundation and
      the Issuer. This is the single structural fix: it gives the company a revenue line, and it
      is the only arrangement that survives an EU transfer-pricing review. Counsel already
      flagged that revenue generation sits with EU entities; an EU company working for a Panama
      principal at zero margin is the textbook challenge.
- [x] **1.3** At an 8% margin the fee is $9,378,339 against $8,683,648 of cost — $694,691 of
      margin, roughly $111,000 of EU tax. **Doing it properly costs about 2.4 months of runway.**
- [!] **1.4** Confirm the margin with counsel. 5–10% is the routine-services band.

## Part 3 · Pool allocation — your insight, sized

- [x] **3.1** The distinction you drew is the right one and it is not in any document yet:

> **BND/USDC attracts third-party liquidity** — anyone wanting BND exposure will provide it.
> **rwaUSD/USDC has no natural outside provider.** The protocol must be the depth, because the
> APSS defends that pool and retail exits route through it.

- [x] **3.2** Options against the $5,950,000 available:

| BND pool | rwaUSD pool | Split | BND side | Pool value |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $500,000 | $5,450,000 | 8 / 92 | 10,000,000 (1.00%) | $1,000,000 |
| **$750,000** | **$5,200,000** | **13 / 87** | **15,000,000 (1.50%)** | **$1,500,000** |
| $1,000,000 | $4,950,000 | 17 / 83 | 20,000,000 (2.00%) | $2,000,000 |

- [ ] **3.3 · Recommend $750,000 to BND.** It sits in the middle of the range you named, puts
      1.5% of supply into the pool — thin but workable for a launch that expects third-party
      LPs — and leaves $5,200,000 where it actually matters.
- [x] **3.4** At $5,200,000 the rwaUSD pool turns over **3.0× a month** at peak volume
      ($15.5M/month by M36). That is a healthy ratio for an AMM; below roughly $3M it would be
      thin enough that APSS corrections start moving the price materially.
- [ ] **3.5** The engine must be changed to split this. Today it routes 100% into BND/USDC and
      the rwaUSD pool has no modelled depth at all.
