Abstract

BOUND is not starting from zero. The Atomic Peg Stabilization System — the rwaUSD settlement dollar and its core liquidity pool — is already deployed and operational. This roadmap extends that foundation to support instant liquidity for real-world assets. It is a migration plan, not a rebuild.

Delivery is structured as twelve milestones across three phases, executed by four engineering layers running in parallel from the first week. Development is budgeted at a fixed $320,000 across twenty-two weeks of engineering execution, against a delivery window of twenty-two to twenty-five weeks to mainnet inclusive of audit remediation and launch. External audit and security-competition fees are budgeted and tracked separately.

This roadmap presents twelve milestones across three phases, taking BOUND from a locked architecture decision through to live RWA markets on mainnet. Each milestone is written for a non-technical audience: what gets built, what becomes possible as a result, and why it matters strategically.

Development is budgeted at a fixed $320,000 across 22 weeks of engineering execution, with a realistic delivery window of 22–25 weeks to mainnet inclusive of audit and launch buffer. External audit and security competition fees are budgeted and tracked separately from the development allocation.

This document defines the complete technical build roadmap for the BOUND Protocol. It covers four parallel engineering layers — Smart Contracts, Backend & Oracle Automation, Frontend, and DevOps & Security — structured across three high-intensity phases targeting mainnet launch in 18 to 25 weeks.

The roadmap delivers every component required for BOUND to go live: the rwaUSD and liquidity pool contracts, the oracle-driven haircut and settlement engine, the RWA issuer onboarding and management system, the instant exit swap interface, and the data infrastructure serving issuers, asset managers, and external integrators.

Compression from the original 36-week plan to 18–25 weeks is achieved entirely through parallel execution across all layers — not by reducing scope. Every deliverable is preserved. Achieving 18 weeks requires dedicated, full-time teams across all four layers from day one.

This document opens with a plain-language roadmap that any reader can follow end to end. The chapters that follow descend into the full engineering specification — every deliverable, every layer, every gate. A reader who needs only the shape of the programme can stop after Chapter 1.

Starting Point and Scope

2.1 What BOUND is

BOUND is the institutional liquidity infrastructure layer for Real-World Assets. Tokenization has perfected asset issuance — but secondary liquidity remains a massive barrier. Allocators hesitate to commit capital when faced with 30-to-90-day redemption windows.

BOUND eliminates that friction by providing a shared, on-chain liquidity layer that gives RWA holders an instant dollar exit via the rwaUSD — while the underlying fund preserves its stable, long-term background settlement timeline. rwaUSD trades at $1.00 on Uniswap and can be swapped for USDC instantly.

2.2 What is already deployed

The APSS system — the rwaUSD settlement dollar and the core liquidity pool — is deployed and operational. This programme extends it rather than replacing it, which is the single most consequential fact about the schedule and the budget: the foundation does not have to be rebuilt, and live markets must not be disrupted while it is extended.

BOUND is not starting from zero. The APSS system — rwaUSD and core liquidity pool — is already deployed and operational. This roadmap extends that foundation to support Real-World Asset instant liquidity, rather than building a new protocol from scratch.

Delivery Principles

Six principles govern how the programme is executed. They are constraints on the delivery team, not aspirations.

3.1 Decentralisation by Default

All normal BOUND operations are permissionless. Admin keys are multi-sig gated and scope-limited to emergency market suspension and haircut parameter bounds only. Emergency controls must self-expire or require community ratification via timelock. Issuers, asset managers, and rwaUSD holders can operate without any reliance on BOUND team keys.

3.2 Parallelism is the Compression Strategy

The timeline shortens entirely through parallel execution. Smart contract development, backend, frontend, and DevOps all run at full capacity simultaneously from Week 1. Any sequential dependency is a schedule risk to be raised at the weekly cross-team sync immediately.

3.3 Audit Gate is Non-Negotiable

No mainnet deployment until Phase 3 audit sign-off. Any contract modification from competition findings triggers a targeted re-audit of the affected modules. The audit window cannot be compressed below 6 weeks without dropping the public competition round, which is not recommended for a protocol handling institutional capital.

3.4 MiCA Compliance from Architecture

The BOUND smart contract and backend architecture is designed around MiCA Title II utility-type classification from day one — not retrofitted. Compliance reporting pipelines, data retention, and issuer obligation tracking are first-class engineering concerns.

3.5 SEO from Day One

Frontend SEO is an engineering deliverable from Phase 1. The target is for BOUND to rank as the primary result for 'RWA instant liquidity', 'instant RWA exit', and 'rwaUSD' before mainnet launch.

3.6 Security at Every Layer

Security is not siloed to smart contracts. DevOps, backend, and frontend each carry their own security obligations — WAF, SAST/DAST, oracle node hardening, HSM-backed hot wallet signing, input validation — throughout all phases.

Audit and Security Strategy

BOUND's contracts handle institutional capital with a programmatic peg mechanism. The audit strategy is non-negotiable and structured in two rounds. Any shortcut here increases existential protocol risk.

7.1 Round 1: First-Party Audit — Weeks 17–20

Engage a top-tier firm (Trail of Bits, Certora, or OpenZeppelin) for full-scope review of rwaUSD, liquidity pool, RWA registry, oracle interfaces, and governance contracts. Remediation sprint runs concurrent with Week 20 — all findings triaged, fixed, and re-tested.

7.2 Round 2: Public Competition — Weeks 21–23

Code4rena or Sherlock competition. Broad researcher coverage on rwaUSD peg mechanics, haircut logic, and oracle manipulation vectors. All valid findings trigger a targeted re-audit of affected modules before mainnet deployment.

7.3 Ongoing: Immunefi Bug Bounty — Post-Launch

Live bug bounty with tiered payouts scaled to finding severity. Any post-launch finding that requires a contract modification triggers an immediate re-audit of the affected module before the fix goes to mainnet. No exceptions.

Audit booking is a Phase 1 actionTop-tier firms — the ones this plan names — quote booking queues of roughly four to twelve weeks from first enquiry to kickoff, and elite-only engagements can run to several months. The audit slot must therefore be secured during Phase 1, not at the end of Phase 2 as the schedule-risk table assumes. Booking late is the cheapest way to lose six weeks on this programme. 8.6 Which entity bears it, and where the IP vests

The programme is executed and paid for by the operating company, incorporated in the European Union, which employs the engineering team and bears all operating cost. It is not borne by the token issuer. The operating company works under a development-and-services agreement with Nexus Technologies Foundation and BITSWIFT TECHNOLOGIES INC., and is reimbursed at cost plus a margin.

The intellectual property this programme creates &mdash; the contracts, the infrastructure and the protocol design &mdash; vests in Nexus Technologies Foundation, which owns the protocol and grants the token delegation. The operating company is a service provider, not the owner of what it builds. Entity roles and the full attribution of every cost line are set out in the Operating Cost &amp; Company Analysis.

The following conditions must hold to achieve 18 weeks. If any are not met, add the indicated buffer to the overall timeline.

The delivery window is twenty-two to twenty-five weeks. An eighteen-week floor is achievable, but only if every condition below holds simultaneously — fully dedicated teams from week one, an audit slot already secured, and a clean single-round audit remediation.
